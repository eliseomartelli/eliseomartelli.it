Why bother?

Why go to that event if people are rude, it's a hassle, and in 10 years I
won't remember most of it anyway?

I know this feeling. It's the cosmic version of nihilism.  
You zoom out too far, and your life starts to look like static.

And yes, objectively, we are small. A blip.

But here's the part that matters: even if you're insignificant to the universe,
you're not insignificant to yourself.

Pain still hurts. Joy still feels good. Meaning still lands.

If someone offers you two lunches, one with your favorite food and one
with something disgusting, you won't be indifferent. You'll pick the better one
immediately.

That tells you everything: your life has value because it's *lived*.

Not because other people care. Because you do.

I don't think life comes with a built-in purpose.  
I think purpose is local, and you write it day by day.

You choose what kind of life you can live with at night.

You choose whether this brief window of consciousness feels more like heaven or
more like hell.

Sometimes when I feel small, I remind myself of this:

I may not change the whole world, but I can still be a spark.  
A conversation, a photo, a gesture, a sentence, a decision. One small thing
that shifts reality by one degree.

Maybe I won't see the effect. That's fine.

We are a blip. An absurdly privileged one.

For a short moment we get color, sound, taste, touch, people, ideas, love,
grief, tension, beauty, boredom, and wonder.

Then it's over.

So no, don't live for "the world" in the abstract.

Live for you.  
Build a life you'd choose again.

My objective is still simple: leave a net positive impact.  
Some days are negative, some days are positive.  
But the direction still matters.
